# OSRS Shipwreck Salvaging Guide 2026: Every Wreck, Level, Hook and Loot Table

OSRS shipwreck salvaging for ironmen: all 8 wreck tiers by Sailing level, hook and station requirements, wiki XP rates, and full sorting loot tables.

21 min read·Updated September 17, 2026·Difficulty: **

Shipwreck salvaging is the Sailing skill's gathering activity: park next to a wreck, drop a hook, pull up salvage, sort it for loot. It is slow XP at low levels and genuinely good XP with the right boat at high levels, and for an ironman the sorted loot is the point. Everything below is from the OSRS Wiki as of **17 September 2026**, including the December 2025 XP review changes.

For Sailing training in general, port tasks, Barracuda Trials and the skill's unlock order, see the [Sailing guide](https://ironman.guide/articles/osrs-sailing-guide). This article covers salvaging only.

## How salvaging works

The loop has two halves, and each gives Sailing XP.

1. **Salvage.** Sail adjacent to a shipwreck and use a **salvaging hook** built on your boat. Salvage range is 7 tiles from the edge of the wreck, measured from the hook, so some wrecks that sit under 14 tiles apart can be worked from one spot. You roll for salvage every **4 ticks** ; each success gives one unsorted salvage and the wreck's salvaging XP.
2. **Sort.** Use the salvage on a **salvaging station** at most ports, or on one built on your boat, to turn it into items and collect the sorting XP. Sorting XP is roughly half the salvaging XP.

Higher-tier hooks and a higher Sailing level both raise your success chance. Salvage goes in your inventory or your boat's **cargo hold**, which does not accept noted items.

**Crewmates** can be told to operate a hook. Their success chance is a fraction of yours that scales with their **deckhandiness**: 10% at deckhandiness 1, 20% at 2, 30% at 3 and 40% at 4, and they roll every 5 ticks instead of 4. Their XP per salvage is reduced the same way, so over time a crewmate earns only a small fraction of what you would on the same hook:

| Deckhandiness | Crewmates | XP per salvage | Success chance | Crew XP as share of yours |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Adventurer Ada, Jittery Jim, Sailor Jakob | 10% | 10% | 0.8% |
| 2 | Oarswoman Olga, Bosun Zarah, Spotter Virginia | 20% | 20% | 3.2% |
| 3 | Jobless Jim (40 Sailing), Ex-Captain Siad (50 Sailing) | 30% | 30% | 7.2% |
| 4 | Cabin Boy Jenkins (60 Sailing), Jolly Jim (85 Sailing) | 40% | 40% | 12.8% |

Crew keep salvaging into the cargo hold while you sort, stop when it is full or no wreck is in range, and resume on their own. **Dragon salvaging hooks can only be operated by deckhandiness 4 crew**, which means Jenkins and Jolly Jim are the only crewmates who can man them.

### Spawn mechanics

Every salvaging area shares a spawn pool: typically 6 wreck sites with 4 active at once (since 5 December 2025 every hotspot has exactly 2 inactive wrecks). The despawn timer for a wreck starts the moment anyone begins salvaging it, and when it sinks another wreck in the area rises immediately. That is why salvaging on the official salvaging worlds (**596 and 597**, both US east) or the unofficial one (486) is faster: other players sinking distant wrecks keeps the ones next to you cycling.

Boosting works, with rules

Wreck level requirements are boostable, but the boost must be visible and must be maintained while you salvage, and your crew stop the moment your boosted level drops below the requirement. The invisible +2 from a keg of whirlpool surprise does not let you salvage above your level (it only improves success rate), and boosts past 99 do nothing. For long sessions the wiki suggests Preserve with prayer-boosting gear and a prayer regeneration potion, or, after clearing the Shark rank of the Tempor Tantrum, a keg of whirlpool surprise on the boat for an unlimited +3 as long as you carry an empty beer glass.

Some tiers sit in hazardous water and your boat needs the right part to be there at all, or you get the message that it is not safe to salvage in hazardous waters:

- **Barracuda:** at least an oak mast (most wrecks also want an iron helm); a camphor mast fully avoids Storm Tempor damage. At least one wreck sits just outside the Storm Tempor and needs neither.
- **Mercenary:** all of them are in tangled kelp, so you need an **adamant helm** .
- **Fremennik:** icy seas, so you need an **eternal brazier** . Ironmen must complete Making Friends with My Arm to make one.
- **Merchant:** eternal brazier for Winter's Edge or an adamant helm for the Southern Expanse.

The reason the jump to 53 matters: below it you are on a skiff with one hook and no station, banking every trip. From 53 you are on a sloop with two hooks, a crewmate on the second one, and a station on deck, which is the setup that makes salvaging semi-AFK. The wiki's procedure is to man one hook yourself with Jenkins (or Jobless Jim before 60 Sailing) on the other, deposit everything into the cargo hold, and when it is nearly full hand your hook to a second crewmate while you sort.

## XP rates

The wiki lists per-setup rates on each wreck page (in the table above) and Jagex published long-form tests after the 5 December 2025 salvaging changes. All crewmates in the tests had deckhandiness 4 and none included crystal extractor XP. Jagex did not say which wrecks were used or whether travel was included.

| Tested Sailing level | Hooks | Method | Sailing XP/hr |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 97 | Two dragon hooks | Low-attention sorting while two crewmates salvaged | 35,000 to 40,000 |
| 87 | Rune hooks | Active salvaging and sorting | 105,000 to 110,000 |
| 97 | Two dragon hooks | Active while multitasking | About 85,000 |

Two multipliers on top of any setup:

- **Tick manipulation** on the hook gives roughly 2 to 2.5 times the XP, at much higher intensity.
- The **crystal extractor** (73 Sailing, 67 Construction, needs a Heart of Ithell) gives 250 XP per wind mote every 109 ticks, which the wiki works out to 13,761 XP/hr if you harvest every time, or 14,103 with a keg of horizon's lure. After Song of the Elves each harvest also has about a 1 in 6 chance of a crystal shard.

The wiki's money-making guides quote salvage per hour of 140 (small), 160 (fisherman's), 220 (Barracuda), 360 (large), 360 (pirate), 315 (mercenary), 310 (Fremennik) and 280 (merchant), assuming crew do a third to two thirds of the hooking.

## Loot by wreck

Every sorted salvage rolls a pre-roll table for uniques first, then one main table. Rates are exact wiki values. Two things every tier shares:

- **Sailors' amulet (inert):** 1/2,000 from small salvage, 1/1,500 fishy, 1/1,000 Barracuda, 1/500 large, 1/450 plundered, 1/400 martial, 1/350 Fremennik, 1/300 opulent. It is never rolled if you already own one, and you can only own one. It teleports to the Pandemonium, Port Roberts, Red Rock and Deepfin Point once you have inspected each Sailors' Marker, uses one charge per teleport, and one law rune plus 10 water runes adds 10 charges (up to 10,000). The Deepfin Point teleport, from 67 Sailing, lands about eight tiles from a bank chest.
- **Salvaging relics** at 1/3,000 from every tier, handed to Curator Haig Halen for 1 kudos and an antique lamp: 5,000 XP (any skill at 30+) for the rusty locket, mouldy block and dull knife; 10,000 XP (level 40+) for the broken compass, rusty coin and broken sextant; 15,000 XP (level 50+) for the smashed mirror and mouldy doll. Repeat hand-ins give 5,000 coins instead.

## Ironman tips

What salvaging is actually for on an iron

- **Skip straight to what your account needs.** Large wrecks (53) for giant seaweed and rings of recoil, mercenary (73) for ranged ammo and an amulet of power, Fremennik (80) for astral runes and prayer XP via troll heads, merchant (87) for seeds, herbs and gems. The tiers below 53 are only for the hook and station unlocks on the way.
- **Sort on the boat, drop the junk.** Once you have the station facility, the best XP comes from never leaving the site: store what you want in a seed box, herb sack, gem bag, plank sack or log basket, alch the jewellery and armour, and drop the rest. Banking everything is slower and the wiki's per-hour figures assume minimal banking.
- **Get the right crewmate early.** Jobless Jim (40 Sailing, deckhandiness 3) makes your second hook worth building; Cabin Boy Jenkins (60) or Jolly Jim (85) nearly double what a crewmate contributes and are the only two who can use dragon hooks.
- **Take the Sailors' amulet seriously.** At 67 Sailing the Deepfin Point teleport is an eight-tile bank teleport that costs a tenth of a law rune per use. Since it never rolls once you own one, every tier gives you a fair shot at it.
- **Hand in every relic.** Eight relics, eight antique lamps: 5,000, 10,000 or 15,000 XP each in the skill of your choice, plus a kudos each.
- **Build the eternal brazier yourself.** Ironmen need Making Friends with My Arm before Fremennik and Winter's Edge merchant wrecks are reachable at all.

Two things that catch people out

Wreck timers start when anyone starts salvaging, not when you arrive, so a wreck someone else touched can sink under you. And a boosted Sailing level has to stay up for the whole session: the second it drops below the wreck's requirement your crew stop, and the whirlpool surprise keg's invisible +2 does not count for unlocking a tier.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is shipwreck salvaging in OSRS?

Shipwreck salvaging is a low-intensity Sailing gathering activity. You park your boat next to a shipwreck, deploy a salvaging hook, and pull up unsorted salvage for a small amount of Sailing XP. Sorting that salvage at a salvaging station at a port, or at one built on your boat, gives roughly half as much XP again plus the actual loot: seeds, herbs, planks, cannonballs, jewellery, relics and a few uniques.

### What Sailing level do you need for each shipwreck?

Small shipwrecks need 15 Sailing, fisherman's shipwrecks 26, Barracuda shipwrecks 35, large shipwrecks 53, pirate shipwrecks 64, mercenary shipwrecks 73, Fremennik shipwrecks 80 and merchant shipwrecks 87. The requirement is boostable with a visible boost, but the boost has to be maintained while you salvage or your crew will stop too.

### Which shipwreck should I salvage at my level?

Small at 15 with a bronze hook, fisherman's at 26 with an iron hook, Barracuda at 35 with a steel hook, large at 53 with two mithril hooks on a sloop, pirate at 64 with two adamant hooks, mercenary at 73 once you have an adamant helm for tangled kelp, Fremennik at 80 once you have an eternal brazier for icy seas, and merchant at 87 with rune hooks. The wiki's money-making guides use exactly those setups.

### What do you need to start salvaging?

Only a raft with a salvaging hook and Pandemonium completed. To do it efficiently you want a sloop with two of the best hooks you can build, a cargo hold, a salvaging station on the boat (42 Sailing to read the schematic, 34 Construction to build it) and a crewmate with high deckhandiness such as Jobless Jim or Cabin Boy Jenkins to operate the second hook.

### How much Sailing XP per hour is salvaging?

It scales hard with the wreck tier and hooks. The wiki lists roughly 1,600 XP per hour on small wrecks with a bronze hook, 5,000 on fisherman's wrecks with iron, 24,000 or more on large wrecks with Jobless Jim and mithril hooks, 45,000 or more on pirate wrecks with adamant hooks, 60,000 or more on mercenary and Fremennik wrecks with rune hooks and Cabin Boy Jenkins, and Jagex measured 105,000 to 110,000 on active rune-hook salvaging at 87 Sailing. Tick manipulation roughly doubles player rates.

### Is salvaging worth it for an ironman?

Yes, for the resources rather than the XP. Large salvage drops giant seaweed and sapphire rings, Fremennik salvage drops astral runes and ensouled troll heads, mercenary salvage drops adamant and rune arrows, dart tips and an amulet of power, and opulent salvage rolls the rare seed, herb and gem tables. Every tier can also give a Sailors' amulet, a relic worth an antique lamp, and clue scrolls.
